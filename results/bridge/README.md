# results/bridge

The numbers behind the bridge-builders figure of the article (papers, authors, institutions) and Tables S1 to S3 of supplement S5. Everything here is written by `python -m neuroai_cn.analysis.export_bridge_tables` from the pipeline parquet outputs under `$NEUROAI_DATA/data/derived/corrections/`, and I made the figure `python -m neuroai_cn.figures.figure2_bridges_v3` read only these files, so the figure and the tables cannot drift apart. `tests/test_bridge_tables.py` pins the values.

`table_s1_papers.csv` is Table S1, the fifteen strongest bridge papers. I rebuild it from the 133,763 papers with at least five classified citations from each side: I keep papers whose own field label is neuro or ai and whose all-time classified citations are under 10,000 (58,971 papers), rank them by the harmonic mean B of the neuroscience and AI citation counts, drop OpenAlex types other than article or review, ebook-platform and book-series sources, empty titles, three book titles I listed by hand and duplicate titles, and take the first fifteen. The script stops if this differs from the input the figure used. `paper_class` is my hand coding behind "about half are tools": seven tools and eight concepts. `s1_eligibility_check.csv` lists the 40 best eligible candidates with the reason each excluded one was dropped. `venue` is the OpenAlex source name and is blank for XGBoost, which OpenAlex records without a source.

`table_s2_authors.csv` is Table S2, the top fifteen authors by summed B over the top 5,000 eligible papers, with at least two papers each. `table_s3_institutions_consolidated.csv` and `table_s3b_institutions_separated.csv` are Tables S3 and S3b, the top fifteen institutions with and without merging Google, DeepMind, Microsoft and Meta sub-entities into parent groups. `institution_ranks_top100.csv` holds the full persisted ranking of both variants, for ranks beyond fifteen such as Meta at 19 or DeepMind at 26. As in the figure, the corporate rows of the consolidated table were aggregated over all 133,763 papers that pass the 5-and-5 gate and the other rows over the top 5,000 eligible papers, so paper counts should be read within a row and not compared across the two kinds of row.

`author_institution_edges.csv` holds the 26 author-to-institution edges I verified by hand (10 current, 16 past) with the rank of the institution in each variant, and whether the edge is drawn. `paper_author_links.csv` holds the eight links between a top-15 paper and a top-15 author that the figure draws, which I recomputed from the OpenAlex authorship lists of the fifteen papers. The pipeline's own link file stops after 100 rows and has no rows for SciPy and XGBoost, but it gives the same eight drawn links.

`concentration_tiers.csv` gives the share of total bridge weight held by the top 1, 5, 10, 15, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500 and 1000 authors and institutions in four pools. `all_133763_unfiltered` covers all 133,763 papers that pass the gate and includes the Large Helical Device fusion block. `all_133763_minus_mean_B_gt_400` is the sensitivity bound behind the article's concentration figures (S5, sections 3.7 and 4.7): the same pool after dropping every author and institution that averages more than 400 units of B per paper, computed from the top-1000 tables. `neuro_ai_59038` and `e2_58971` hold full distributions for the papers with a neuroscience or AI label and for the eligible set of Table S1. `headline_numbers.csv` has one number per row: pool sizes, tool and concept counts, the rank of Aude Oliva, edge counts, top-100 shares and Gini coefficients.
