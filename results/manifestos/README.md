# Do the NeuroAI manifestos follow the citation curve of the overall flow?

A reviewer of the Transmitter piece asked whether citations of the NeuroAI manifestos follow the same curve as the overall flow, and whether AI researchers are influenced by them. I resolved the six manifesto and review papers of the article sidebar by DOI in the OpenAlex mirror (snapshot 2026-03-30), and all six resolve (`resolved_works.csv`). Four are labeled neuroscience by the extended classifier (Hassabis 2017, Richards 2019, Lillicrap 2020, Macpherson 2021) and two are labeled AI (Sinz 2019, Zador 2023); the author labels in the tables are mine, the titles come from the mirror. The definitions are in the docstring of `src/neuroai_cn/analysis/manifesto_citations.py`, and `python -m neuroai_cn.analysis.manifesto_citations --recompute` re-queries the mirror (about ten minutes) while the plain command rebuilds the derived tables from the committed counts.

Citations received between 2020 and 2023 (`totals_2020_2023_by_citing_field.csv`), by the citing paper's own field label, AI / neuroscience / neither: Hassabis 2017 341 / 206 / 288; Richards 2019 153 / 365 / 140; Lillicrap 2020 201 / 208 / 122; Sinz 2019 41 / 54 / 32; Macpherson 2021 8 / 17 / 35; Zador 2023 16 / 19 / 21. Summed, the six received 760 citations from AI papers and 869 from neuroscience papers in those four years. I kept one edge dated 2012, before any of the six existed, although it is an OpenAlex date error. Per year, the citations from AI papers to the six together are 3 (2017), 32, 71, 144, 215 (2021), 194, 207 (2023), 185 (2024) and 214 (2025), against 57,081 AI-to-neuroscience citation edges in 2020 rising to 72,841 in 2023 and 97,295 in 2025 (`share_of_flow_by_year.csv`, `citations_by_paper_year_field.csv`). The six papers therefore account for 0.25 to 0.32 percent of the AI-to-neuroscience flow in 2020-2023, peaking in 2021, and for 0.2 percent in 2024-2025: their citations rose steeply to 2021 and then flattened while the flow kept rising.

When I remove the six as cited works, the restricted odds ratio of AI papers citing neuroscience changes by at most 0.31 percent in any year from 2015 to 2023 (2023: 0.0544 to 0.0543; `or_excluding_manifestos.csv`), and the neuroscience-citing-AI series by at most 0.02 percent. In the shape comparison over 2015-2023 (nine year-over-year changes in log of one plus the count) I find a Spearman correlation of 0.59 between the manifesto citations from AI papers and the number of AI-to-neuroscience citation edges (exact permutation p = 0.098) and of -0.34 with the odds ratio (p = 0.36). The two level series correlate at 0.98. But that only reflects that both rise over the period.

What I conclude: descriptively, the six papers have been cited by AI papers at a rate that rose from 2017 to 2021 and has been roughly flat at about 200 a year since, a minority share of well under 1 percent of the flow, and they do not drive the odds-ratio series. What I cannot conclude: the correlations rest on nine changes, one series is zero for the first two of them, and no exact p-value is significant; six papers cannot support a test of whether the curves are the same, and a citation from an AI paper is no evidence of influence (reviews are cited for many reasons, and these are also cited by neuroscience papers about as often). The odds ratio mixes the size of both fields and is not designed to track single papers. The 2024-2025 values are provisional because of the OpenAlex rebuild window. I answered the question here as a description, not as a test.
