# Results

Small, committed result files that the figures, supplements and tests are built from. Everything here is CSV. Here I describe `flow/` and `crosssource/`, the citation-flow inputs and outputs behind article Figures 1 and 2 and the change-point numbers; `citation_age/` is described where its code lives.

## Direction naming

The data column `direction` has two values. `neuro_to_ai` means neuroscience papers citing AI; the article labels this flow AI -> Neuro (knowledge moving from AI into neuroscience) and draws it in gold. `ai_to_neuro` means AI papers citing neuroscience; the article labels this flow Neuro -> AI (knowledge moving from neuroscience into AI) and draws it in teal. In every file the field named first is the citing field, so the counts are references made by that field's papers in the given citing year.

## Provenance

Source: OpenAlex snapshot of March 2026 (about 458 million works), extended AI definition. I exported the two inputs that cannot be rebuilt without the full pipeline once, unchanged, with `python -m neuroai_cn.analysis.export_flow_inputs` (I copied the values with their shortest round-trip representation and edited nothing by hand). Everything else is recomputed from `flow/or_by_year.csv` by the figure scripts (`python -m neuroai_cn.figures.figure1_main_v2 or`, `python -m neuroai_cn.figures.figure2_raw_v2`), by `python -m neuroai_cn.analysis.changepoints` and by `neuroai_cn.analysis.flow_numbers`. The CSV bundles regenerated here are byte-identical to the data bundles of Figures 1 and 2 of 2026-10-06. `NEUROAI_FLOW_SOURCE=heavy` makes the loaders read the parquet and `cells.jsonl` files from `NEUROAI_DATA` and `NEUROAI_CELLS_ROOT` instead of these CSVs.

## flow/or_by_year.csv

The input table: 72 rows, one per citing year (1990 to 2025) and direction, 20 columns. `year` is the citing year. `n_to_within` is the number of references made by the citing field's papers to papers of the same field, `n_to_target` references to the other field (the cross-field citations), `n_to_other` references to works classified in neither field, `n_to_unknown` references to works with no field assignment, `n_to_missing` references to works absent from the database; all are counts of references. `rho_restricted` is `n_to_target / (n_to_within + n_to_target)` and `rho_null_restricted` the share expected from field sizes alone, `n_works_target / (n_works_source + n_works_target)`. `rho_population` is `n_to_target / (n_to_within + n_to_target + n_to_other + n_to_unknown)` and `rho_null_population` the expected share with the third-field papers added to the denominator. `or_restricted` is the odds ratio of `rho_restricted` against `rho_null_restricted` (1 means chance; it is the quantity plotted in Figure 1) and `or_restricted_lo`, `or_restricted_hi` its Wilson 95% interval; `or_population` with `_lo`, `_hi` is the population variant. `n_works_source`, `n_works_target` and `n_works_other` are the numbers of papers in a given year in the citing field, the other field and all remaining fields. All odds ratios are dimensionless.

## flow/figure1_article_panelA.csv, _circles.csv, _panelB.csv, _panelC.csv, _markers.csv

The values plotted in article Figure 1 (`figures/figure1_article.png`), written by `python -m neuroai_cn.figures.figure1_main_v2 or` together with the figure. `figure1_article_panelA.csv` has one row per year (1995 to 2025) and line: `odds_ratio_yearly`, `band_wilson95_lo/hi` (the pale band), `thick_line_5yr_centred_mean` (the thick line: centered 5-year mean of the yearly values over 1995 to 2025, at least 3 points, so 1995 and 2025 average 3 years and 1996 and 2024 average 4, as `thick_line_n_years_averaged` says), the reference counts, the observed and expected shares and the field sizes behind each value; `provisional_year` is true for 2024 and 2025. `figure1_article_circles.csv` has the four open circles (2024 and 2025, both lines): the odds ratio from the May 2026 re-download (`crosssource/openalex_live_extended.csv`, 1,500 papers per field and year) computed against the snapshot's expected share, its Wilson interval (not drawn), the sample counts, the snapshot's yearly value and thick-line value at the same year, their ratio, and the per-1,000 values of both with the sample's bootstrap interval. `figure1_article_panelB.csv` is panel B: the OLS slope of the yearly odds ratio per year inside the four eras of `style.CANONICAL_ERAS`, clipped to 1995 to 2023, with bootstrap percentile intervals (`ci95_*` are the whiskers, `ci99_*`; B = 500, seed 20260528), the fraction of bootstrap slopes above and below zero and the marker printed in the figure (`**`, `*` or `n.s.`). `figure1_article_panelC.csv` is panel C: the same for the segments between the change points. `figure1_article_markers.csv` lists the shaded eras, the dashed change points, the milestone papers and the hatched band, with the x-range each covers as drawn. Slope units are odds ratio per year.

## flow/companion_counts_shares_sizes.csv and figure2_raw_v2_data.csv

The numbers behind Figure 2 and the text, one row per year (companion) or per year and line (Figure 2). The companion table has `neuroscience_papers` and `ai_papers` (papers per year in each field), `citations_neuroscience_citing_ai` and `citations_ai_citing_neuroscience` (cross-field references per year, counts), `cross_refs_per_1000_refs_*` (cross-field references per 1,000 references recorded, denominator including unknown-field references, that is `rho_population` x 1000) and `refs_per_*_paper` (references recorded per paper). `figure2_raw_v2_data.csv` holds the plotted values: `A_cross_field_citations` (count) with its thousands version, and `B_cross_refs_per_1000_references_yearly`, whose denominator is own plus other plus third-field references (unknown-field references excluded), so it is up to 0.43 percent higher than the companion column; `*_5yr_centred_mean_PLOTTED` are the thick lines and `total_references_recorded` is the Figure 2B denominator.

## flow/changepoints.csv and segment_slopes.csv

Written by `python -m neuroai_cn.analysis.changepoints` from `or_by_year.csv` only. `changepoints.csv` is tidy, one row per breakpoint (a single row with empty breakpoint columns when a run finds none). Columns: `procedure` (`piecewise-OLS exhaustive`, the search the figure uses, or `PELT (ruptures)`), `setting` (window rule, minimum segment, cost, penalty), `series` (`or_restricted` is the plotted yearly odds ratio; `or_population` and `log_or_restricted` appear only in the exploratory PELT block), `direction`, `citing_field`, `window` (inclusive years analyzed), `declared_in_advance` (false marks exploratory runs), `n_years`, `n_breakpoints`, `breakpoint_rank`, and the breakpoint in two conventions: `breakpoint_year_first_of_new_segment` (the year the figure labels) and `breakpoint_year_last_of_old_segment`, always one year earlier. `segment_slopes.csv` gives for every run and segment the OLS slope of the series per year (odds ratio per year), its standard error, the 95% t interval (n - 2 degrees of freedom) and R2. I fixed all PELT settings before running: cost l2 on the series standardized to mean 0 and standard deviation 1 within the window, minimum segment 3 years, penalty per segment equal to c x ln(n) with sigma squared = 1 (BIC c = 1, 2xBIC c = 2) or the constant 2 (AIC-like), n the number of years in the window.

## crosssource/

One CSV per cross-source `cells.jsonl` sample, exported unchanged: `openalex_live.csv` (strict definition) and `openalex_live_extended.csv` (extended) are my OpenAlex re-download of May 2026, `s2.csv` is Semantic Scholar, and `fresh_recheck_20261006_n1500.csv` and `fresh_recheck_20261006_n1500_2010_2019.csv` are my October 2026 re-check samples. One row per direction and year with the sampled `n_papers`, reference counts `within`, `target`, `other` (same meaning as above), `n_refs_classified` (their sum), `per1000` (cross-field references per 1,000 classified references) and its Wilson interval `ci_lo`, `ci_hi`. The Semantic Scholar file also carries sampling bookkeeping (`n_sampled`, `n_elided`, `elision_rate`, `n_unreachable`). The article figures use these only for the cross-source panels of the supplement. But the article Figure 1 draws the snapshot alone.

## flow/sensitivity_strict_or_by_year.csv, sensitivity_loto.csv, fresh_recheck_field_sizes_20261006.csv, fresh_vs_snapshot_2020_2025.csv

Robustness checks of the Figure 1 odds ratio, quoted in supplement S2 and written by `python -m neuroai_cn.analysis.export_flow_sensitivity` from the heavy derived data (I edited nothing by hand). `sensitivity_strict_or_by_year.csv` is the whole snapshot table recomputed with the 35-topic AI core I curated (`AI_CORE_TOPIC_IDS`) instead of the extended AI definition: same 20 columns and meanings as `or_by_year.csv`, 72 rows. `sensitivity_loto.csv` is the leave-one-topic-out sweep: for each of the 27 candidate topics (`topic_added`, an OpenAlex topic URL) that I re-admitted into AI one at a time, the yearly odds ratio of each direction (`or_loto`), the published value (`or_canonical`, identical to `or_restricted` in `or_by_year.csv`), their difference (`delta`) and the relative change (`pct_change`, a fraction: 5.06 means +506 percent). `fresh_recheck_field_sizes_20261006.csv` gives papers per year in the October 2026 OpenAlex vintage (`neuro`, `ai_extended` = subfields 1702 plus 1707, `ai_strict` = subfield 1702). `fresh_vs_snapshot_2020_2025.csv` sets the October 2026 sample (`crosssource/fresh_recheck_20261006_n1500.csv`, 1,500 papers per field and year) against the snapshot for 2020 to 2025: `fresh_per1000` / `snap_per1000` cross-field references per 1,000 references, `*_refs_per_paper` references recorded per paper, `*_restricted_share` cross-field share of own-plus-cross references, `*_null` the cited field's share of the two fields' papers, `*_OR` the restricted odds ratio. I recompute the fresh columns from committed files only and check them against the original working table when it is present.

## flow/field_sizes_by_year.csv, ai_topic_sizes_by_year.csv, ai_works_2000_2004_breakdown.csv, subfield_sizes_2000_2004.csv

Field sizes behind the odds ratio and supplement S1, written by `python -m neuroai_cn.analysis.export_field_sizes` from aggregate passes over the `works` table of the DuckDB index (I edited nothing by hand). `field_sizes_by_year.csv` is long: one row per publication year (1990 to 2026; 2026 is incomplete) and `definition`, with `n_works` (works whose primary topic falls in the definition, any work type), `n_with_references` (those with a non-empty reference list) and `n_article_or_review` (OpenAlex type article or review). Definitions: `all_works`, `with_primary_topic`, `unknown` (no topic), `neuroscience` (field 28), `ai_extended` (the article's AI: subfield 1702 minus 26 topics, plus subfield 1707, plus topic T10502), `ai_strict_core35` (the curated 35-topic core), `ai_subfield_1702_whole`, `ai_1702_minus_excluded`, `ai_subfield_1707`, `ai_topic_T10502`, `excluded_26_topics`, `excluded_T12157` and `other_extended`. The `neuroscience` and `ai_extended` rows equal `n_works_source`/`n_works_target` of `or_by_year.csv` in every year, and `ai_strict_core35` equals the AI field size of `sensitivity_strict_or_by_year.csv`. `ai_topic_sizes_by_year.csv` gives works per year for every topic of subfields 1702 and 1707 and for T10502, with `status` (`excluded`, `extended_and_core35`, `extended_only`). `ai_works_2000_2004_breakdown.csv` breaks the extended-AI works of 2000 to 2004 down by `dimension` (venue, DOI registrant, work type, venue recorded, reference list present, OpenAlex record-creation year, publication date given as 1 January), one row per key with `n_2000` ... `n_2004` and the 2001-to-2002 increase; venues and registrants keep the 25 largest increases and pool the rest. `subfield_sizes_2000_2004.csv` gives works per year 2000 to 2004 for every OpenAlex field and every subfield with at least 5,000 works in 2001, with the 2002/2001 ratio. All counts are numbers of works.

## corpus/

Corpus-level numbers for supplement S1, written by the same script. `corpus_totals.csv`: one row per `quantity` (records in the parquet download, works in the DuckDB index, works with a topic or a reference list, citation links, topics) with a plain-language `definition`. `classification_topics.csv`: every topic that the field definitions touch (field 28, subfields 1702 and 1707, topic T10502, and the neighboring subfields 3205, 3206 and 1708 that I deliberately left out), with `label_extended`, `label_strict_core35`, a `note` for excluded or neighboring topics and `n_works_1990_2025`. `pool_sizes.csv`: the four pools I curated (`kind` = `pool_size`), the exact overlap partition (`exclusive_combination`, works that are in exactly that set of pools) and the Pool 1 split into anchor signatories (1a) and institutes (1b). `anchor_labels.csv`: the 70 rows of my May 2026 anchor audit (68 distinct papers) with the expected label, the OpenAlex primary topic, subfield and field, and the labels under the extended topic scheme, whole subfield 1702, the venue lists and Semantic Scholar fields of study.

## flow/flow_composition_topic_year.csv, flow_composition_head.csv, flow_concentration.csv

What neuroscience papers cite when they cite AI (the gold flow), quoted in supplement S4 and written by `python -m neuroai_cn.analysis.export_flow_composition` from small files that `neuroai_cn.audits.flow_composition` and `neuroai_cn.audits.flow_concentration` left under `DERIVED_DIR` (I edited nothing by hand; the export never touches the mirror). `flow_composition_topic_year.csv` has one row per `classifier` (`extended`, the article's AI definition, or `strict`, the curated 35-topic core), `citing_year` (1990 to 2025), cited AI `topic_id` and `topic`, with the `category` I assigned by hand (six categories, defined in `CATEGORIES` of `neuroai_cn.figures.flow_composition_fig`) and `n_cites`, the number of citation edges from neuroscience papers of that year to AI works of that topic. Its yearly totals equal `n_to_target` of the `neuro_to_ai` rows of `or_by_year.csv` (extended) and `sensitivity_strict_or_by_year.csv` (strict); the export stops if they do not. `flow_composition_head.csv` lists the 200 AI works most cited by neuroscience papers over citing years 1990 to 2025, extended definition, with `rank`, title, publication year, topic, category, `n_cites` and the running total `cum_cites`. `flow_concentration.csv` gives, for the same edges, how many distinct cited AI works (`n_works`, and as a fraction of all cited AI works, `share_of_works`) carry 50, 80, 90 and 95 percent of the citations (`quantile_of_citations`), with the totals `total_edges` and `n_distinct_cited_works`.
